How a mandate runs

From first call to transfer

Four stages, and the seller knows which one the property is in at any point.

The walk and the schedule

We walk the farm and work through the eighteen section schedule together: extent, arable split, boreholes and yields, dams, licences, buildings, power, security, equipment and zoning. Anything unknown is recorded as unknown.

Pricing and mandate

Price is set against what comparable land has actually transferred for in that district, not against what the neighbour is asking. The mandate, commission and VAT treatment are agreed in writing before anything is published.

Marketing

The listing goes live on erfdevco.com and on Property24 with the same photography and the same schedule of facts in both places, so a buyer comparing the two never finds a discrepancy.

Offer and transfer

Offers are put in writing with their suspensive conditions spelled out. From acceptance we keep the conveyancer, the bank and both parties working off one set of facts until registration.

Why the schedule

A farm is a business, so it is listed like one

Residential portals ask about bedrooms and bathrooms, because that is what they were built for. A buyer looking at four hundred hectares needs the borehole yield, the carrying capacity, the water use licence status and the zoning before the photographs mean anything.

The ERFDEVCO listing schedule runs to one hundred and three fields across eighteen sections. It is the same on every farm, which means two listings can actually be compared, and a bank or a valuer can work from the page rather than starting again from scratch.
