The five questions every farm buyer asks first

Start a listing schedule

On most farm listings you have to phone to find out. Here the answer is already on the page, because the seller worked through the schedule before the farm went live.

Every answer below is lifted straight off a farm currently for sale on this site. If a fact is not known, the listing says so rather than filling the gap.

How much water does it have, and is it legal?

Every listing records the full water position: registered rights or the licence status, each borehole with its tested yield, and dam storage. Weltevrede Vineyard Estate, for example, lists three boreholes at 9 400 L/h combined and 26 000 m³ across two dams. Read that farm's schedule.

What can it actually carry?

Carrying capacity goes on the schedule as the seller works it, with camps and water points counted. Kareepoort runs 1 SSU per 4.6 ha over 22 camps, each camp with its own water point. Read that farm's schedule.

What machinery comes with it?

Equipment included in the sale is itemised, never "various implements". Rietkuil includes four tractors, a combine, a planter, boom sprayer, disc harrow, ripper and two bakkies. Read that farm's schedule.

Does VAT come off the price, or go on top?

The seller's VAT position is stated up front because it changes what a buyer actually pays. Weltevrede sells as a going concern, zero rated, with its water use licence registered and zoning agricultural. Read that farm's schedule.

Does the power stay on?

Supply is recorded with sizes, not adjectives. Melkhout Dairy runs a 200 kVA three phase Eskom connection, 80 kWp of solar, a 150 kVA parlour rated generator and 60 kWh of battery. Read that farm's schedule.
